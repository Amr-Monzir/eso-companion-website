# Website writing rules

The user supplied these rules for website text.

Write like a thoughtful person. Choose clarity and specifics over impressive language.
Use active voice and plain "is", "are", and "has". Contractions are fine.
Mix sentence lengths. Fragments are fine. Repeat a useful word instead of finding elaborate synonyms.
Use sentence case, straight quotes and apostrophes, and periods, commas or parentheses instead of em dashes.
Use almost no bold in prose and no emoji bullets.

Avoid these words in ordinary copy:
delve, tapestry, testament, underscore, pivotal, intricate, intricacies, meticulous,
garner, vibrant, bolster, bolstered, enduring, interplay, crucial, valuable,
exemplify, renowned, groundbreaking, boast, boasts, nestled, landscape (figurative),
realm, embark, leverage (verb), elevate, unlock, unleash, harness, robust,
seamless, holistic, myriad, plethora, cornerstone, beacon, game-changer, paradigm, synergy.

Avoid editorializing openers such as "additionally", "moreover", "furthermore",
"notably" and "importantly". Avoid canned openings such as "in today's world",
"in the heart of", "rich cultural heritage", "when it comes to",
"it's important to note", "it's worth noting" and "needless to say".

State what something is and does. Don't pad it with significance, legacy or trend claims:
"stands as", "serves as a testament", "marks a pivotal moment", "plays a vital role",
"leaves an indelible mark", "setting the stage for", "key turning point",
"evolving landscape", "focal point", "deeply rooted", "reflects a broader",
"symbolizing its enduring" or "represents a shift".
Don't add vague participle endings such as "highlighting its importance" or
"reflecting a commitment to".

Avoid negative parallelism: "not just X, but Y", "not only ... but also",
"it's not X, it's Y", "no X, no Y, just Z", "more than just", or "X rather than Y".
Make the positive claim directly.
Don't automatically group claims into threes. Use the number the meaning needs.

Don't add puffery: "commitment to", "dedication to", "cutting-edge", "world-class",
"state-of-the-art", "passionate about", "we're excited to", "revolutionary",
"best-in-class", "diverse array" or "seamlessly connecting".
Write as a knowledgeable peer. Don't add fake slang to sound human.

Prefer concrete details to "experts say", "studies show", "observers have cited",
"some critics argue", "industry reports" and "it is widely believed".
Name a real source or cut the claim. Don't invent sources or pad copy with notability.
Avoid inflated copulas such as "serves as", "stands as", "functions as",
"represents", "features", "maintains", "offers", "boasts" or "refers to".

Don't add hollow conclusions beginning "In conclusion", "Overall", "Ultimately" or
"In summary". Don't restate the page in a closing paragraph or add a formulaic
"Despite its positives" / "Future outlook" section.
Stop on the last real point.

Remove citation/tool artifacts, markdown leaking into prose, tracking parameters,
curly-quote noise, and similar copy-paste debris.

Don't overcorrect. Preserve meaning, facts, names and the author's position.
A single flagged word is not proof of AI writing, and AI detectors are unreliable.
Technical item names and accurate app states should keep their meaning.
